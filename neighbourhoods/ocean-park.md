    <h1 class="intel-header">Ocean Park: The Coastal Monopoly</h1>
    <p class="intel-sub">Supply-constrained living where geography creates the ultimate barrier to entry.</p>
  </header>

  <section>
    <p>Ocean Park is the only neighborhood in South Surrey that offers a true village atmosphere combined with high-value coastal assets. It is a market defined by scarcity. Because there is no more land being created on the bluff, you aren't just buying a home—you're buying a position in a geographic monopoly.</p>

    <h2 class="section-title">The Advisor Audit</h2>
    <p><strong>The Asset:</strong> Detached executive homes, often on 8,000 to 12,000 sq ft lots. The value is in the land and the proximity to the village core.</p>
    <p><strong>The Risk:</strong> Geotechnical stability and 2026 drainage requirements. I review the specific subsidence history of any bluff-side property before subject removal.</p>
    <p><strong>Resale Logic:</strong> Ocean Park remains the top choice for local downsizers and families who value walkability. This internal demand creates a faster-than-average resale velocity.</p>

    <h2 class="section-title">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
    <h3 class="metric-label">What is a Geotechnical Covenant on the Ocean Park bluff?</h3>
    <p>Most bluff-front properties carry a restrictive covenant requiring specific engineering setbacks and foundation designs. I audit these titles to ensure you aren't inheriting a "buildable area" debt that limits your renovation or reconstruction plans.</p>

    <h3 class="metric-label">Is Ocean Park considered a supply-constrained market?</h3>
    <p>Yes. Geography acts as the ultimate barrier to entry here. With the ocean to the south and limited transition to high-density zoning, Ocean Park functions as a geographic monopoly. This creates a defensive floor for property values even during broader market corrections.</p>
